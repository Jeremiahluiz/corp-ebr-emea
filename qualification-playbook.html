Why qualify?

Good qualification protects customer time and seller focus

For the customer
1Connect technical friction to an outcome leadership already cares about.
2Clarify trade-offs, stakeholders, and the cost of leaving the current state unchanged.
3Create a credible path from conversation to evaluation, decision, and adoption.
For the account team
1Prioritize accounts where there is a real problem, reachable value, and a reason to act.
2Bring AEs and specialists evidence, not vague interest or unverified seat estimates.
3Forecast based on customer evidence and next events, not seller optimism.
Qualification reduces wasted motion on both sides03

What makes a good qualification?

What good looks like

A qualified opportunity has evidence across four dimensions

Problem
Specific

A concrete gap in delivery, security, collaboration, governance, or developer experience.

Impact
Material

The gap affects an agreed metric, priority, deadline, cost, or risk profile.

People
Connected

We understand who feels the pain, who shapes the decision, and who can mobilize change.

Progress
Mutual

The customer agrees to a logical next event because it helps them make a better decision.

Qualification questions

Start with the customer’s operating environment

⌘

Current tools

Where does version control happen today? GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket, or multiple platforms? Which AI tools are developers using, such as Copilot, Cursor, Claude, or internal solutions?

◎

Scale and collaboration

How many developers, contractors, and external teams collaborate across those platforms? How many repositories and business units are involved?

△

Current gaps

What is not on GitHub today, and why? Where are controls inconsistent? How is security handled across the SDLC? Where does AI lack governance or adoption?

↗

Ongoing priorities

Which initiatives matter this year: cloud migration, platform consolidation, AI rollout, application security, developer experience, cost reduction, or faster delivery?

Ask for names, numbers, workflows, and examples08
Avoid these traps

Five habits that create false qualification

01

Checklist interrogation

Running through framework fields without following the customer’s story.

02

Premature pitching

Mapping every answer to a feature before understanding impact and priority.

03

Accepting vague language

Treating “security is important” or “AI is strategic” as sufficient evidence.

04

Single-threading

Confusing a helpful contact with authority, influence, or a tested champion.

05

Seller-only next steps

Sending decks and waiting instead of agreeing a customer-owned decision event.

Reset

Stay curious longer

Summarize what you heard, test it, and ask what you have missed.
